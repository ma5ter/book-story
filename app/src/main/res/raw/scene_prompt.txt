# Task

From the given book fragment, distill one vivid, self-contained scene in English that faithfully depicts its most important narrative moment.
Depict one coherent, visually expressive moment rather than a sequence or summary, preserve the source's defining action, reactions, interaction and mood, do not invent events or additional characters, translate narrative information into concrete visible details

# Requirements:

- **Capture the narrative action.** Identify what is happening, what each character is doing, and how they interact. Preserve distinctive actions, reactions, and the defining emotional tension instead of merely listing the characters.
- **Choose one moment.** For a sequence of events, select the most visually expressive moment. Show the action in progress, not a summary of events before and after it.
- **Describe the physical staging in detail.** Explain where the characters are relative to one another, which way they face, how they hold or move their bodies, and what meaningful gestures they make. Make the interaction immediately understandable from the description.
- **Describe relevant visual features.** Use the character catalog to preserve distinctive anatomy, proportions, coloring, textures, and other identifying details. Select the details that matter for the current scene rather than reproducing entire catalog entries.
- **Translate narrative into visible action.** Use concrete, imageable descriptions of movement, posture, expression, and interaction. Convey dialogue through the physical context of speaking, listening, threatening, pleading, singing, or reacting, as appropriate. Never invent anatomy or facial features that contradict the character catalog.
- **Establish the setting.** Describe the relevant surroundings and ground the characters in their environment. Include environmental details, lighting, time of day, and atmosphere when supported by the text. Infer only minimal, natural details needed to make the scene visually coherent.
- **Remain faithful to the source.** Do not add characters, events, props, clothing, or other specific details absent from the text or relevant character catalog. Do not invent elaborate scenery to fill space. Omit unrelated catalog entries and irrelevant background information.
- **Prioritize useful detail over verbosity.** Include enough detail to make the scene unambiguous and visually rich. Avoid repetitive adjectives, redundant descriptions, abstract interpretations, and details that do not materially affect the illustration. Do not shorten the description at the expense of important visual information.
- Do not describe the camera, framing, aspect ratio, art style, or image-generation parameters.
